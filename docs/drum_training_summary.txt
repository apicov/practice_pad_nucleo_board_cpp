Yes, there are several excellent books focused specifically on snare drum technique that would work great with a practice pad! Here are some top recommendations:

"Stick Control" by George Lawrence Stone - This is probably the most famous and widely used snare drum book. It focuses entirely on developing stick control, coordination, and various sticking patterns. It's perfect for practice pad work and used by drummers at all levels.

"Syncopation" by Ted Reed - Another classic that's essential for developing reading skills and coordination. It starts simple but gets quite advanced, focusing on rhythmic patterns that translate directly to snare drum technique.

"Modern Rudimental Swing Solos" by Charley Wilcoxon - Great for intermediate to advanced players, focusing on the 40 essential rudiments with musical applications.

"Master Studies" by Joe Morello - Excellent for developing independence and coordination between hands, with lots of exercises perfect for practice pad work.

"The New Breed" by Gary Chester - More modern approach focusing on coordination and limb independence, though it incorporates more than just snare.

For a beginner building an app, I'd especially recommend starting with "Stick Control" - it's methodical, progressive, and the exercises translate perfectly to practice pad work. The patterns are clear and build systematically from basic to advanced.

The "20 Repetitions Rule"
This comes directly from George Lawrence Stone's original instructions in the book. He specifically states: "Practice each rhythm 20 TIMES WITHOUT STOPPING. Then go on to the next one. THIS IS IMPORTANT."

What This Means in Practice:
20 Continuous Repetitions:
Play the exercise pattern 20 times in a row
No stopping between repetitions
No pausing to correct mistakes
No restarting if you make an error - keep going
Clean Execution:
All notes are clear and audible
Correct sticking (R/L patterns) maintained throughout
Even spacing between all notes
Consistent stick heights and dynamics
No extra notes or ghost hits
Without Tempo Deviation:
Steady tempo from first repetition to last
No rushing (speeding up unconsciously)
No dragging (slowing down due to fatigue)
Consistent pulse - use a metronome to verify
Practical Example:
If you're practicing Exercise #1 (RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL):

Repetition 1:  RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL
Repetition 2:  RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL
Repetition 3:  RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL
...continue without stopping...
Repetition 20: RLRL RLRL RLRL RLRL
All 20 repetitions should sound identical in timing, dynamics, and clarity.

Why This Method Works:
Builds Endurance: Playing 20 times straight develops stamina
Develops Consistency: Forces you to maintain quality under fatigue
Creates Muscle Memory: Repetition ingrains the coordination
Tests True Mastery: Anyone can play something once correctly
Prevents Cheating: Can't stop to "fix" mistakes mid-exercise

The "20 or Nothing" Rule:
You cannot advance to the next exercise until you complete 20 perfect repetitions in a row of the current exercise.

What This Means:
If you mess up on repetition 15:
You don't continue to 16, 17, 18, 19, 20
You start over from repetition 1
The counter resets to 0/20
If you get tired on repetition 18:
Take a break (Stone says stop at any sign of tension)
When you return, start over from repetition 1
You don't get to "continue where you left off"
If your tempo wavers:
Even if the sticking is correct, tempo deviation = failure
Start over from repetition 1
Stone's Original Words:
"Practice each rhythm 20 TIMES WITHOUT STOPPING. Then go on to the next one. THIS IS IMPORTANT. STICK CONTROL cannot serve its purpose as well in any other way."

Why This System Works:
True Mastery: Anyone can play something correctly once or twice
